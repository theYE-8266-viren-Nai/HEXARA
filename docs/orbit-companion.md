# ORBIT companion

ORBIT is OrbitWell's floating robot AI companion, available in all three workspace stages. The user's robot image has been cut out onto a transparent PNG at `public/images/orbit-companion.png` using the built-in image-editing tool. It is served locally in the launcher and chat header, supports light/dark themes, and stops moving with reduced motion. `ChatWidget` retains an optional `avatarSrc` override.

Image edit brief: remove the entire blue/white-noise and black background; preserve the original robot, pose, expression, coat, stethoscope, antenna ring, colors, and fine edges; use genuine alpha transparency, including gaps, and keep the full robot uncropped.

Tap the floating pet to open a conversation beginning with "How are you feeling?" Users can describe symptoms or ask everyday questions without choosing a condition. ORBIT shows listening, thinking, or support states. Urgent help starts a message in the same conversation rather than opening a separate blood-sugar-focused panel. Privacy and first-aid references are expandable.

Replies show at most 30 words in 1–3 short lines, with longer fixed guidance and references collapsed under "More guidance". Urgent escalation and key cautions remain in the visible message. The header states that no one has been contacted. Cloud prompts target 10–20 words; server and browser checks reject generated replies over 30 words, 250 characters, or three nonempty lines through existing retry/fallback behavior. Older cloud replies outside this contract get a brief notice. Greetings, fallback messages, and status copy are also shorter.

`supabase/functions/_shared/emergencySupport.ts` provides fixed conversational guidance for breathing/choking, chest symptoms, sudden neurological changes, bleeding, allergy, seizure, unresponsiveness, poisoning/overdose, burns, injuries, heat/cold exposure, glucose, personal safety, illness, medication questions, and mission hazards. Unfamiliar urgent concerns get general escalation guidance. `chatSafety.ts` shares this routing with the repository and Edge Function while retaining the output wording guard. This broad keyword matching is not exhaustive and must never be presented as emergency detection or triage.

Matched symptom conversations bypass cloud generation and stay in browser-session memory. Follow-ups such as "I'm alone", "No", and "What should I tell the medic?" retain their local context; a newly reported urgent concern can replace the earlier topic. Urgent messages work while history or AI is pending, and an older pending AI reply is discarded after a local support exchange starts. Users explicitly return to everyday chat to leave this context; earlier symptom messages are not added to cloud requests.

ORBIT is not a doctor, medical device, emergency monitor, or clinically validated triage tool. It does not measure blood glucose, infer actual conditions from simulated readings, select medication or doses, or contact responders. Urgent replies direct users to the crew medic, mission emergency channel, or local emergency services on Earth and explicitly say nobody has been contacted. Earth first-aid references do not replace onboard mission protocols or medical instructions.

Regular questions retain the existing cloud chat and account persistence. Sharing a dashboard summary is off by default and resets when stage/account changes. Network failures get explicitly labelled built-in responses. Both current and historical assistant output are checked in the browser; the Edge Function also checks new generated output. These conservative text checks are additional safeguards, not proof that arbitrary model output is medically safe.

Blood-sugar wording is based on [NIDDK low-blood-glucose guidance](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/preventing-problems/low-blood-glucose-hypoglycemia) and [CDC treatment guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/treatment/treatment-low-blood-sugar-hypoglycemia.html), reviewed November 13, 2026. Insulin lowers blood glucose and is not a treatment for suspected low blood glucose. The companion directs users to their clinician-provided plan and crew medical support without selecting medication or doses.

Other public references are attached to relevant chat replies: [MedlinePlus first aid](https://www.medlineplus.gov/firstaid.html), [NHS emergency support](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/urgent-and-emergency-care-services/when-to-call-999/), and NHS/Red Cross pages for the specific concern. The support catalog contains the exact URLs reviewed November 13, 2026. Mission-environment replies refer to approved onboard procedures rather than inventing operational steps.

The frontend works locally with the existing Vite server. The updated `chat-assistant` Edge Function has not been deployed by this change; use the existing deployment instructions in `docs/explain-setup.md` when deploying. No database schema change is needed. Real emergency use requires qualified clinical and mission-protocol review beyond this synthetic research demo.
