# Postflight wellbeing and the overview effect

This note informs the Retired astronaut experience in OrbitWell. It is research context for a synthetic demo, not a clinical protocol.

## What the evidence says

**The overview effect is a reported experience, not a diagnosis.** Yaden and colleagues describe astronauts' accounts of awe, emotion, and connection with Earth and humanity after viewing Earth from space. Their 2016 paper proposes psychological concepts for studying the experience; it does not establish that every astronaut has it or that it causes a mental health condition. [Yaden et al., *Psychology of Consciousness*](https://doi.org/10.1037/cns0000086)

**Many people try to put the experience into words with others.** Rush analyzed 51 public astronaut interviews, then corroborated patterns with five more astronaut interviews. The account describes initial speechlessness, later sensemaking, and attempts to communicate meaning. Those are patterns in selected accounts, not a screening test or a universal sequence. [Rush, *Journal of Applied Behavioral Science*](https://doi.org/10.1177/00218863221136289)

**Interpretations differ.** Weibel's ethnographic work used first-person writing and 14 interviews with nine astronauts. It reports profound changes for some astronauts, but not all, and shows that culture and prior beliefs shape interpretation. The product should never tell a person what their own view of Earth *must* have meant. [Weibel, *Religions*](https://doi.org/10.3390/rel11080418)

**Postflight adjustment deserves human support in its own right.** NASA's Behavioral Health and Performance program describes psychological support and family assistance across training, missions, and postflight, including work with former astronauts. A NASA behavioral health technical brief describes preparation for repatriation and support for reintegration with family and work when needed. NASA's 2024 behavioral health presentation names family, sleep, and meaning in post-mission reintegration support. These sources support giving people simple paths to reflect and reach a person; they do not establish that a chatbot can provide therapy. [NASA Behavioral Health and Performance](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/jsc-behavioral-health/) · [NASA behavioral health technical brief](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/behavioral-health-technical-brief-ochmo.pdf) · [NASA 2024 presentation](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240014687/downloads/BHP%20for%20Future%20Space%20Exploration%202024.pdf)

## Product choices

- After the first-time pairing step, show one Mental friend card that opens ORBIT chat. A device pattern cannot establish emotional wellbeing.
- Keep the simulated wearable summary below the card on Home, with deeper charts in Trends and private check-ins in Wellbeing.
- Let the person choose whether to discuss the Earth view, post-mission routine, relationships, or something else. Do not presume an overview effect or distress.
- Use reflective, open questions and avoid claiming ORBIT understands, monitors, contacts someone, diagnoses, or treats.
- Keep urgent support routing and a visible path to a real person. Conversation and self-reflection must not delay help when there is immediate danger.
- State when a reply is built in because cloud AI is unavailable; do not imply it was generated or saved.

## Limits

The astronaut interview literature has small, selected samples and often relies on retrospective or public accounts. The overview effect and postflight adjustment should be studied further. This demo does not measure mental health, validate a therapy, or replace a clinician or trusted person.
